Abstract

Modern financial risk management increasingly deploys continuous probabilistic machine learning architectures and Large Language Models (LLMs) to automate regulatory auditing, compliance monitoring, and high-frequency trade execution. However, an epistemological conflict exists between continuous optimization spaces and discrete, hard regulatory boundaries. This paper proves the Neuro-Symbolic Impossibility Theorem in Automated Financial Governance, establishing that no purely probabilistic continuous neural architecture can simultaneously guarantee non-zero semantic coverage over unobserved market regimes and zero probability of catastrophic safety boundary violation. To resolve this structural limitation, we introduce Project Alethea, a decoupled tri-engine neuro-symbolic framework. We formulate Kinetic Risk Entropy (KRE) to bound liquidity cascade dynamics, establish spectral radius stability conditions ($\rho(\mathbf{A}) \le 0.85$) on interbank exposure matrices, enforce subadditive Expected Shortfall ($\text{ES}_\alpha$) controls, and model non-linear regime transitions using René Thom's Cusp Catastrophe theory.

1. Introduction, Epistemological Foundations, and the Governance Paradox

Enterprise banking architectures and automated market operations are rapidly adopting deep autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs), multi-modal attention networks, and deep reinforcement learning agents to streamline compliance auditing, liquidity routing, and covenant verification under modern regulatory frameworks (such as Basel III/IV, Fundamental Review of the Trading Book standards, and central bank technology risk policy documents). A prevailing technological hypothesis in modern financial engineering asserts that scaling deep neural networks will eventually eliminate automated compliance errors, resolve reporting bottlenecks, and guarantee real-time risk monitoring safety through sheer parameter expansion and broad semantic training.

This monograph demonstrates that this scaling hypothesis is fundamentally flawed due to an intractable epistemological conflict between continuous optimization spaces and discrete regulatory mandates. While neural networks excel at extracting implicit semantics from multi-modal, unstructured financial datafeeds, their continuous, non-convex loss surfaces inherently preclude formal verification against hard regulatory and operational boundary constraints.

Executive Epilogue: Practitioner’s Perspective on Automated Governance

Note: This epilogue provides an applied governance commentary by the author, grounding the paper’s mathematical formalisms in real-world institutional risk practice.

In my professional practice as a Chief Risk Officer across major banking institutions, I am continually confronted by a structural disconnect between data science engineering teams and institutional risk governance committees. Machine learning teams naturally focus on optimizing semantic coverage, reducing training loss, and expanding the generative capabilities of agentic systems. Conversely, banking regulators, internal audit boards, and risk committees do not evaluate risk through smooth loss surfaces; they operate strictly under zero-tolerance constraints regarding tail-risk containment, capital preservation, and statutory policy compliance.

This operational reality provided the primary motivation for Project Alethea. Probabilistic neural architectures cannot serve as unconstrained sovereign decision-makers in high-stakes financial environments. By decoupling semantic market interpretation from deterministic mathematical verification—forcing every candidate action generated by neural estimators to pass through an unyielding Symbolic Veto Kernel—we establish an institutional bridge between machine learning adaptability and deterministic accountability.
